Pricing

How the number gets set

Three steps, and you can stop after any of them at no cost.

1. A free consultation

Thirty minutes on what you actually need and what it has to connect to. Nothing is quoted on the spot, and there is nothing to sign.

2. A written scope

Everything the build includes, listed out, so you can see exactly what you would be paying for before you see a price at all.

3. One fixed price

Quoted against that scope and held to it. You approve it before any work starts, and you are free to take it elsewhere.

Where each starting figure comes from

Hours a week

The one task you already know eats your week: the inbox, the intake form, the spreadsheet you retype into. Six is a round placeholder, roughly an hour a working day. We count yours properly during the audit on the first call.

Hours left after

Automation rarely takes a task to zero. Someone still reviews the exceptions, so an hour stays on the books rather than us promising you the whole six back.

What the hour is worth

Your number, not a market rate we picked: the wage plus payroll cost of whoever does the task today, or what you would have billed a customer for that same hour.

Build, plus a year of support

Whatever your written quote says. We do not publish prices, so until you have one, put in the most you would be comfortable spending and see whether the hours cover it.

Where the line is

Worth saying out loud before you hand anyone the keys to your business.

An assistant that says "I don't know"

A chatbot that invents an answer about your pricing or your hours costs you more than having no chatbot at all. Ours are built to hand off to a human rather than guess, and you can read every conversation it has.

Your accounts stay yours

Domain registered in your name, code in a repo you own, hosting under your email. We never buy a domain on your behalf or hold your accounts hostage. Leaving should cost you nothing but a goodbye.

Method

Small improvements, shipped continuously.

Kaizen means exactly that. Nothing here takes a quarter to deliver, and you see working output early enough to change your mind while it is still cheap.

How a project runs

Four steps. Most projects are done inside a month.

01
Free, 30 minutes

The call

We go through what you have now and where the time goes. No deck, no pitch. If a website is not your actual problem, this is where that comes out.

02
Within a few days

Scope and fixed price

You get it in writing: what gets built, what it costs, when it lands. One number, agreed before anything starts. Nothing gets added to the invoice later without you approving it first.

03
Usually 1 to 3 weeks

Build and demo

You get a live link, not a mockup. Click through it on your phone, mark up anything that feels wrong, and we revise. This is the point where changes are easy, so we push for honest feedback rather than polite approval.

04
Launch day and after

Launch and hand over

Domain connected, certificate live, forms tested. Then you get access to everything and a short walkthrough of how to update it. Ongoing support is optional, never automatic.

Why it works this way

You see it early

A demo link on day seven beats a finished site on day thirty that missed the point. Feedback while it is cheap is the entire reason for the schedule.

Fixed price protects you

Hourly billing rewards slow work. A number agreed up front means the incentive is to build it well and be done, and you know the cost before you commit.

Handover is part of the job

You should be able to change your own hours, prices and photos without paying anyone. Anything we cannot make self serve, we document.

Questions we get

Who owns the site and the domain?

You do. The domain is registered in your name on your account, the code lives in a repo you own, and hosting is under your email. We only ever need access, never ownership.

What if I already have a website?

Most clients do. We rebuild it, carry over the copy and photos worth keeping, and cut over with minimal downtime. Your email keeps working throughout.

Can I update it myself afterward?

Yes, and you get a walkthrough at handover. If you would rather not, a monthly care plan covers edits.

Do I need the AI parts?

Probably not on day one. Most businesses get more out of a better site and one automation than out of a chatbot. We will tell you which is which.

How do payments work?

Half to start, half at launch, for most projects. Monthly plans are month to month with no contract.

About Us

A small shop that ships, not a firm that presents.

tsKaizen is an Orlando based AI and web studio. The pattern behind everything here is the one the name points at: find the friction, build the system, automate it, then scale it.

How we work

Founded and run by Anthony Pichardo. The person you talk to on the first call is the person who designs it, builds it and hands it over.

The background is operations: process design, workflow automation, and the unglamorous work of making information move without a person carrying it. That has meant building a full operating system for a 300 plus member organization, with attendance, points and communications automated end to end, and shipping a tournament platform that ran with thirty concurrent live users.

tsKaizen applies that to small businesses. A cafe, a detail shop, a photographer and a bookstore all have the same underlying problem: too much of the week goes to work that a system should be doing.

Small by design
