The Data

Your permit record is public. Almost nobody reads it.

The City of Orlando publishes permit applications, issued permits, and business tax receipts as open data. Joined together, they show how long your jobs actually take to clear review compared with everyone else in your trade.

The datasets

DatasetIDWhat we use it for
Business Tax Receipts7388-4re5Business name, owner name, phone, address, license type, open date, status. Filtered to contractor license categories.
Permit Applicationsryhf-m453Who applied, for what scope, at what valuation, and when.
Building Permits Issuedk5wb-xgjcIssue dates. Paired with application dates this gives the cycle time.

What we compute

Median days from application to issue, by trade and by jurisdiction. Then your own last twenty permits against that median, and against the fastest quartile in the same category.

What we verify first

Orange, Seminole and Osceola publish separately with different completeness, and field definitions differ. Every number gets checked against the source definition before it goes in front of a contractor. Being wrong about someone's own data is not recoverable.

Why this does not fix itself

Central Florida's construction labor shortage means firms cannot hire their way out of a backlog of paperwork. The people who could clear it are the same people you cannot find. That is the gap the agents sit in.

The hedge

If construction softens, bid work gets more valuable, not less. Orlando financial activities already shed 3,100 jobs year over year and consumer spending is off 2.9 percent. Fewer jobs on the board means every bid matters more, which is why the Bid Agent leads.

Also tracking: Florida SIRS

Structural integrity reserve studies are required for condo and co-op buildings of three or more habitable stories, with deadlines running through December 31, 2026.

Provider waits run three to six months in some markets and studies run $5,000 to $25,000 and up. Non compliance exposes boards to personal liability, insurance denial, and lending restrictions.

The engineering work requires a licensed engineer. The documentation and compliance tracking around it does not. Property management companies buy that across dozens of associations at once.

If you manage associations

Say so on the call. The compliance tracking piece is a different build from the contractor agents and we will tell you honestly where it stands rather than sell you a roadmap.

Method

Service first. Software when it has earned it.

Kaizen means small improvements, made continuously, by the people doing the work. The engagement is built that way on purpose: deliver by hand, record every correction, automate only what has proven itself twice.

The first ninety days

A real sequence, not a phase diagram.

01
Weeks 1 to 2

Read the record

Pull the public permit and license data, compute cycle time by trade, and build your line against the median. In parallel, the bid chain gets drafted against real invitations to bid, not sample documents.

02
Weeks 3 to 4

One bid, by hand, in under a day

You send one live ITB. We do not ask for a meeting first. The teardown comes back inside twenty four hours, done manually, and every correction you make gets recorded word for word. Then one question: what would you have paid to have this by Friday?

03
Weeks 5 to 8

Bid Sprint

A two week paid pilot at $2,500. Delivered by hand, no interface. Between deliveries we build the ingestion and retrieval layer against your real documents, so the system is trained on your work rather than on a demo set.

04
Weeks 9 to 12

Convert to a license

Sprints convert to a monthly license with ongoing tuning. The Permit Index goes public. Referral partners get brought in, usually a permit expediter or a construction attorney, because that is where the next five clients come from.

Why by hand first

Corrections are the asset

Every edit you make to a draft is a training signal that a generic tool throws away. Delivering manually for the first five clients is how the system gets good by month six.

You get paid work, not a pilot

A deliverable you can send beats a demo you have to imagine using. Revenue and product development happen in the same motion instead of one waiting on the other.

Custom work does not eat the product

Nothing gets built that cannot be reused for the next client, and integrated builds carry a hard floor. That discipline is the difference between a product and a very slow agency.

Questions worth asking

Who owns the data?

You do. Your documents, your outputs, your corrections corpus. If the engagement ends, the record leaves with you.

Are you interpreting code?

No. Outputs are flagged and cited so a licensed professional can confirm or overrule them. Licensed sign off stays in your contract, where it belongs.

Does anything get submitted automatically?

Never. No automatic submission to any jurisdiction, at any stage, on any plan.

What if we already use Procore, Autodesk, or Sage?

Good. Integrated builds are quoted separately, with a hard floor, and only after the standalone version has proven out on your work.

How fast is the first deliverable?

The first bid teardown comes back within twenty four hours of you sending the ITB.
