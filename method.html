Step by step

What actually happens, in order.

No mystery, no month of silence. Here is the whole engagement, from first message to the system running on its own.

Step 01 · Advise

The free consultation

You tell me what is slowing you down. I ask how customers actually reach you today, what happens when nobody picks up, and where the day disappears. Nothing to prepare, and no obligation at the end of it.

Step 02 · Advise

The audit and the plan

I go through your site, your response times, your intake, and your follow-up the way a customer would. You get a written plan naming the biggest leak, the fix, what it costs, and what it should be worth. If the honest answer is that you do not need me yet, that is what the plan says.

Step 03 · Build

Design and approval

You see the design and the conversation flows before anything is built. We adjust on paper, where changes are free, and agree on exactly what launch includes.

Step 04 · Build

Build and test

I build the site, train the assistant on your business, and wire the automations to the tools you already use. Then I try to break it: odd questions, bad phone connections, weird form entries, edge cases at 2am.

Step 05 · Build

Launch and handover

We go live in stages so nothing disrupts your operations, and I walk you and your team through it in plain language. You own your accounts, your data, and your domain. Always.

Step 06 · Improve

Watch the first weeks closely

Real customers ask things nobody predicts. I read the actual conversations, fix the gaps, and tune the assistant to how your customers really talk.

Step 07 · Improve

The monthly loop

Every month: what changed, what improved, what is next. New automations get added as needs appear, and the assistant gets retrained as your business changes. Small improvements, compounding, which is precisely what Kaizen means.

Principles

The rules I work by.

The leak comes before the tool

I do not lead with technology. I find what is costing you customers, then pick the smallest thing that fixes it. Sometimes that is not a rebuild.

You own everything

Your domain, your accounts, your data, your customer conversations. No hostage situations, no platform you cannot leave.

Plain language, always

You should be able to explain your own system to a friend. If I cannot describe it without jargon, I have not designed it well enough.

Ship early, improve forever

A good system live this month beats a perfect one launched next year. Then it gets better every month it runs.

A human is always reachable

The assistant handles the repeatable part and hands off the moment a conversation needs a person. Smarter systems, with a human touch.

Honest advice, even when it costs me

If the highest-impact fix is something you can do yourself in an afternoon, I will tell you that instead of selling you a project.

Common questions

About the process.

What do I need to prepare for the consultation?

Nothing. Bring what frustrates you. If you have numbers on missed calls or leads, great, but the conversation works fine without them.

How involved do I have to be?

Heaviest at the start, where you tell me how the business really runs, and at design approval. After launch, the point is that the system runs without you.

What if I want changes after launch?

Expected, and built into the improve stage. Small refinements are part of the monthly loop, and anything larger gets scoped and priced up front.

Can I stop the monthly plan?

Yes. The site and systems stay yours, running on your own accounts. Ongoing improvement is a choice you keep making, not a contract you get stuck in.
